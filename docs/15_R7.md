# 15 — R7: the sharp per-face bound, degree d, cubic min traces, Kitaoka

Roadmap R7 (ledger L5.6): "degree 3 (t₃ ≤ 3; enumerate T_δ vectors of norm ≤ 3), rank 3, Kitaoka connections".

**Answer.**
- **Sharp per-face lemma (PROVED, Theorem 15.1).** For a classical lattice (B(M, M) ⊆ O, the convention of this repo) and any δ ∈ O^{∨,+}, at most rd represented elements have Tr(δα) = 1. Equality forces (M, T_δ) ≅ Z^{rd}, M unimodular and δO = O^∨.
  - Real quadratic consequence: **S(K,2) ≤ 4s**, and S(K,2) ≤ 3s when N(ε) = +1.
  - The bound is attained: D = 82 has s = 1 and S = 4, with (M, T_δ) ≅ Z⁴.
  - This replaces the bound 12s of L1.5, which used kissing numbers; those are needed only for non-classical lattices (C30).
- **Degree d (PROVED, Theorem 15.5).** The number of vectors with T_δ(v) ≤ t is bounded uniformly in M, by packing. L5.6's remark that it is "not uniform in the lattice" is wrong (C31). The field enters only through t(K) and the number Φ_t(K) of functionals.
- **t₃ ≤ 3 is false (C32).**
  - The complete scan of the 832 totally real cubic fields with discriminant ≤ 20000 gives t(K) ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5}. The first t = 4 is at disc 3137 and the first t = 5 at disc 14229.
  - In Thomas's family x(x − a)(x − b) − 1, t(K) ≥ a whenever Z[ρ] = O_K (PROVED, Theorem 15.6). This is CERTIFIED for every 3 ≤ a ≤ 100, so sup t(K) ≥ 100 over cubic fields.
  - t₃ = ∞ is HEURISTIC. The gap: infinitely many monogenic parameter pairs.
- **Kitaoka (PROVED, Corollary 15.8).**
  - A classical universal rank-r lattice over a real quadratic field forces u_E + 1 ≤ 2r on every edge; for ternary forms, u_E ≤ 5.
  - The indecomposables alone cannot prove finiteness in degree 2: in Q(√(n² − 1)) every indecomposable is a power of η (Proposition 15.9).

## 1. The sharp per-face lemma

Setting:
- K is totally real of degree d, with different 𝔡 and codifferent O^∨ = 𝔡⁻¹.
- M is a classical (B(M, M) ⊆ O), totally positive definite O-lattice of rank r, with volume ideal 𝔳M ⊆ O.
- For δ ∈ O^{∨,+} put T_δ(v, w) := Tr(δB(v, w)). This is an integral, positive definite, symmetric bilinear form on M ≅ Z^{rd}.

**Theorem 15.1 (PROVED).**
- (a) The vectors with T_δ(v) = 1 are ±e₁, …, ±e_k with T_δ(e_i, e_j) = δ_ij. Moreover (M, T_δ) = Z^k ⊥ M′, and k ≤ rd.
- (b) det(M, T_δ) = N(δ𝔡)^r · N(𝔳M), a product of positive integers. Hence k = rd forces (M, T_δ) ≅ Z^{rd}, δO = 𝔡⁻¹ and 𝔳M = O (M unimodular).
- (c) #{α ∈ Q(M) : Tr(δα) = 1} ≤ k ≤ rd.

*Proof.*
- (a) Let v ≠ ±w have norm 1. Cauchy–Schwarz gives |T_δ(v, w)| < 1, and T_δ(v, w) ∈ Z, so T_δ(v, w) = 0. The e_i span a unimodular Z^k, which splits off: x − Σ T_δ(x, e_i)e_i is orthogonal to every e_i, with integral coefficients. Orthonormal vectors are independent, so k ≤ rd.
- (b) The discriminant of the trace form of the K-lattice (M, δB) is N_{K/Q}(δ^r 𝔳M)·|d_K|^r. For free M it is N(δ^r det G)·d_K^r, by the standard computation with a Z-basis ω_i e_j; in general it is the same with 𝔳M. Since |d_K| = N𝔡, this equals N(δ𝔡)^r N(𝔳M). Here δ𝔡 ⊆ O and 𝔳M ⊆ O.
  - If k = rd, then Z^{rd} ⊆ M has full rank and is unimodular, so it equals M and det = 1.
- (c) If Q(v) = α with Tr(δα) = 1 then T_δ(v) = 1. Distinct α come from distinct pairs ±v. ∎

For non-classical lattices (B(M, M) ⊆ ½O, Q(M) ⊆ O), 2T_δ is even and the norm-1 vectors are minimal vectors of an even lattice of minimum 2. So L1.4's ½τ_{rd} is the right bound there, and only there.

**Corollary 15.2 (PROVED; sail structure IMPORTED as in L1.5).** Let K = Q(√D).
- Let E be the sail edge between consecutive totally positive convergents α_i and α_{i+2} = α_i + u_E·μ_E, where μ_E = α_{i+1} is the intermediate convergent (N(μ_E) < 0).
- Its functional δ_E is the dual of α_i in the Z-basis {α_i, μ_E} of O, and N(δ_E𝔡) = |N(μ_E)|.

Then:
- (a) A classical rank-r lattice represents at most 2r elements of E. It represents at most 2r − 1 unless N(μ_E) = −1 and M is unimodular.
- (b) N_r(K) ≤ 2r·s. In particular S(K,2) ≤ 4s, and S(K,2) ≤ 3s when N(ε) = +1 (s even).
- (c) S(K,2) ≤ 4s ≤ 8 + 8.32·(Reg(K) − log(√D − 1)). This uses s ≤ 2 + (log ε − log(√D − 1))/log φ (L1.6).
- (d) T(K) ≤ 2s (Proposition 14.4(b) with the rank-1 lattice (𝔟₀⁻¹, γ₀x²), which is classical).

*Proof.*
- **Basis and dual.** Consecutive convergents form a Z-basis of O (p_k q_{k+1} − p_{k+1} q_k = ±1). Writing ν = α_i and d = νμ′ − ν′μ, the dual of ν is δ_E = μ′/d.
- **Norm.** d² is the discriminant Δ of that basis, which is the discriminant of O. So |N(δ_E)| = |N(μ_E)|/Δ, while N𝔡 = Δ.
- **(a)** Apply Theorem 15.1(b). Equality needs |N(μ_E)| = 1, i.e. N(μ_E) = −1. That is impossible when N(ε) = +1. In the data it happens on some edge exactly when s is odd (D < 300, test).
- **(b)** As in L1.5, there are s edges mod (O^×)², and Q(εv) = ε²Q(v). When N(ε) = +1, every edge carries at most 2r − 1 represented elements (3 for r = 2).
- **(c)** Substitute the bound on s.
- **(d)** The rank-1 lattice has rd = 2. ∎

**Checks (CERTIFIED).**
- **Equality at D = 82.** The R1 extremal lattice of Q(√82) is the frame [[10 − √82, −4], [−4, 10 + √82]] glued by (1, 0, 1, 0). Its edge functional is δ = 1/2 + 9√82/164.
  - (M, T_δ) has determinant 1 and exactly 4 pairs of norm-1 vectors.
  - Their values are 10 − √82, 46 − 5√82, 118 − 13√82 and 154 − 17√82 (test_D82_equality_case).
- **Every certified record satisfies S ≤ 4s.** This covers R1 (D ≤ 100 and the long periods), R2 (r2_scan*), R3 (r3_scan_1e6) and edge_stats.
  - max S/s = 4 at D = 82, then 3 at D = 85.
  - Per-edge maxima over all maximal lattices with D ≤ 100: 4 when s is odd (D = 10, 26, 58, 65, 74, 82, 85), and only 2 when s is even, against the proved 3. This is EMPIRICAL (OPEN: is it ≤ 2 for s even?).
- **The edge functional norm.** N(δ_E𝔡) = |N(μ_E)| holds on every edge for squarefree D < 300 (test_edge_functional_norm).

**Remark (norm-2 vectors).** For t = 2, the norm-2 vectors of Z^k ⊥ M′ are the ±e_i ± e_j together with the roots of M′, which form an ADE root system (standard, IMPORTED). Hence
  #{v ≠ 0 : T_δ(v) ≤ 2} ≤ max_k (2k² + R(rd − k)),
where R(m) is the largest root system of rank m (E₆: 72, E₇: 126, E₈: 240, D_m: 2m(m − 1)). For rd = 6 this is 72, attained by Z⁶ and by E₆.

## 2. Degree d: uniform per-functional counts

**Lemma 15.4 (PROVED, packing).** Let (Λ, T) be an integral positive definite lattice of rank n. Then
  #{v ∈ Λ : T(v) ≤ t} ≤ (2√t + 1)^n, uniformly in Λ.

*Proof.* For v ≠ w in the set, T(v − w) ≥ 1. So the T-balls of radius ½ around them are disjoint, and they all lie in the ball of radius √t + ½. Compare volumes. ∎

**Definitions.**
- For α ∈ O⁺: m(α) := min{Tr(δα) : δ ∈ O^{∨,+}}.
- t(K) := max of m(α) over α ∈ I(K). This is finite: I(K)/O^{×,+} is finite, and m(uα) = m(α) for u ∈ O^{×,+} (replace δ by u⁻¹δ).
- Φ_t(K) := min |Δ| over Δ ⊂ O^{∨,+} such that every α ∈ I(K) has some δ ∈ Δ and u ∈ O^× with Tr(δu²α) ≤ t.
  - Φ_t(K) < ∞ iff t ≥ t(K).
  - For K real quadratic, t(K) = 1 and Φ₁(K) ≤ s.

**Theorem 15.5 (PROVED).** Let c(n, t) := sup over integral lattices of rank n of #{v ≠ 0 : T(v) ≤ t}; then c(n, t) ≤ (2√t + 1)^n − 1, c(n, 1) = 2n, and c(6, 2) = 72. For classical M of rank r and every t ≥ t(K),
  s□(M) ≤ Φ_t(K) · ½ c(rd, t).

*Proof.*
- Each represented class contains a represented indecomposable α = Q(v).
- Choose δ ∈ Δ and u ∈ O^× with Tr(δu²α) ≤ t. Then w = uv has T_δ(w) ≤ t and Q(w) = u²α lies in the same class.
- Count the w up to sign, one functional at a time. ∎

In degree 2 this is Corollary 15.2(b), with t = 1 and Φ₁ = s.

So L5.6's claim that "#{v : T_δ(v) ≤ t_d} … is not uniform in the lattice" is wrong (C31). The per-functional factor depends only on rd and t. The field enters through t(K) and Φ_t(K). Section 3 shows that t(K) is not bounded on cubic fields, which is the real obstacle.

## 3. Degree 3: min traces

`scripts/r7_cubic.py` computes m(α) exactly:
- γ = δα ≫ 0 with Tr γ ≤ t has Tr(γ²) ≤ t². So enumerate the lattice αO^∨ with Gram matrix Tr(xy), using LLL and then qfminim (exact integers; PARI's floating version at 200 digits when the integers overflow, with every candidate re-checked exactly).
- Total positivity is tested exactly: a real-rooted char poly has only positive roots iff its coefficients alternate in sign.
- t is raised until there is a hit.
- Indecomposables mod O^{×,+} and facets come from sails (pav10/sails, used read-only).

Cross-checks:
- t = 1 on 15 real quadratic fields (t₂ = 1).
- t = 2 on simplest cubic fields (IMPORTED Kala–Tinková: min traces are 1 or 2).
- An independent LLL-box enumeration of αO^∨ for the extreme α of six fields (§3.3).

### 3.1 Thomas's family

f(x) = x(x − a)(x − b) − 1 = x³ − (a + b)x² + abx − 1 (Thomas 1979, IMPORTED as the source of the family). Let ρ be a root and K = Q(ρ). The elements ρ, ρ − a and ρ − b are units.

**Theorem 15.6 (PROVED).** Let a ≥ 3, b ≥ 2a − 1 and α := 1 + (a − 1)(b − ρ). Then:
- (i) α ≫ 0, and α is indecomposable in Z[ρ];
- (ii) m_O(α) ≥ a for every order O ⊇ Z[ρ];
- (iii) m_{Z[ρ]}(α) = a, attained at δ₀ = (ρ² − (a + 1)ρ + 1)/f′(ρ).

*Root bounds.* Let ρ₁ < ρ₂ < ρ₃ be the roots.
- **Location.** f(0) = f(a) = f(b) = −1, f(1) = (a − 1)(b − 1) − 1 > 0 and f(a/2) = (a²/4)(b − a/2) − 1 > 0. Hence ρ₁ ∈ (0, 1), ρ₂ ∈ (a/2, a) and ρ₃ > b.
- **(R1)** ρ₁ = 1/((a − ρ₁)(b − ρ₁)) < 1/((a − 1)(b − 1)) ≤ 1/8.
- **(R2)** λ := a − ρ₂ = 1/(ρ₂(b − ρ₂)) < 2/(a(b − a)) ≤ 1/3.
- **(R3)** τ := ρ₃ − b = 1/(ρ₃(ρ₃ − a)) < 1/(b(b − a)).

*Codifferent.*
- Z[ρ]^∨ = f′(ρ)⁻¹Z[ρ] (Euler, IMPORTED standard). So δ = g(ρ)/f′(ρ) with g = wx² + vx + u ∈ Z[x].
- The signs of f′(ρ_i) are +, −, +. So δ ≫ 0 ⟺ g(ρ₁) > 0 > g(ρ₂) and g(ρ₃) > 0 ⟺ w ≥ 1 and g has roots r₁ ∈ (ρ₁, ρ₂), r₂ ∈ (ρ₂, ρ₃). (If w ≤ 0, g cannot take the signs +, −, +.)
- Tr(ρ^j/f′(ρ)) = 0, 0, 1, a + b for j = 0, 1, 2, 3. Hence, with k = a − 1 and e := wa + v ∈ Z,
  Tr(δα) = w − k·e, and r₁ + r₂ = a − e/w.

*Proof of (ii).* Let δ ≫ 0 be as above.
- **e ≤ −1.** Tr(δα) ≥ 1 + k = a.
- **e = 0.**
  - From r₂ > ρ₂ we get r₁ = a − r₂ < λ.
  - u = w·r₁r₂ is a positive integer, so 1/w ≤ r₁(a − r₁).
  - x(a − x) is increasing on (0, a/2), so r₁(a − r₁) < λ(a − λ) = ρ₂(a − ρ₂) = 1/(b − ρ₂).
  - Hence w > b − ρ₂ > b − a ≥ a − 1, i.e. Tr(δα) = w ≥ a.
- **e ≥ 1.**
  - From r₁ > 0 and r₂ > ρ₂ we get e/w < λ, so w > e/λ = e·ρ₂(b − ρ₂).
  - Then Tr(δα) > e(ρ₂(b − ρ₂) − k) ≥ (a − ⅓)(a − 1) − (a − 1) = (a − 1)(a − 4/3) ≥ a − 1.
- **Other orders.** O ⊇ Z[ρ] gives O^∨ ⊆ Z[ρ]^∨, so the minimum over O^∨ is at least the minimum over Z[ρ]^∨. ∎

*Proof of (iii).* g₀ = x² − (a + 1)x + 1 has the right signs:
- g₀(ρ₁) > 1 − (a + 1)/((a − 1)(b − 1)) > 0;
- g₀(ρ₂) = −ρ₂(a − ρ₂) − (ρ₂ − 1) < 0;
- g₀(ρ₃) > 0, since ρ₃ > b > a + 1 exceeds both roots of g₀.

Here e = −1, w = 1, so Tr(δ₀α) = 1 + k = a. ∎

*Proof of (i).* Put θ := b − ρ, so Z[θ] = Z[ρ]. Its conjugates are θ₁ = b − ρ₁ ∈ (b − 1, b), θ₂ = b − a + λ and θ₃ = −τ.
- **Positivity.** α = 1 + kθ has α₃ = 1 − kτ > 0, since kτ < (a − 1)/(b(b − a)) < 1. So α ≫ 0.
- **Set-up.** Suppose α = β + γ with β, γ ≫ 0, and write β = x + yθ + zθ² with x, y, z ∈ Z. Then 0 < β_i < α_i.
- **Lagrange.** z = Σ_i β_i/P_i with P_i = ∏_{j≠i}(θ_i − θ_j), where P₁ = f′(ρ₁) > 0, P₂ = f′(ρ₂) < 0 and P₃ = f′(ρ₃) > 0.
- **z ≤ 0.** z < α₁/P₁ + 1/P₃. Using P₁ > (a − 11/24)(b − 1/8), P₃ > (b − 1/8)(b − a) and α₁ < 1 + (a − 1)b, the inequality α₁/P₁ + 1/P₃ < 1 reduces to
  (23/24)a − 285/192 > (a − 11/24)/(a − 1).
  This holds at a = 3 (1.39 > 1.27), and its left side increases while its right side decreases.
- **z ≥ 0.** z > −α₂/|P₂|. Using |P₂| > (a − λ − ρ₁)(b − a + λ), the inequality α₂ < |P₂| reduces to (1 − λ − ρ₁)(b − a + λ) > 1, and (13/24)·2 > 1.
- **Hence z = 0.** Then |y| = |β₁ − β₂|/(ρ₂ − ρ₁) < (1 + (a − 1)b)/(a − 11/24) ≤ b, so |y|τ < 1/(b − a) ≤ ½.
- **x ∈ {0, 1}.** β₃ = x − yτ ∈ (0, 1) forces x ∈ {0, 1}.
  - x = 0: β₃ > 0 forces y < 0, and then β₁ = yθ₁ < 0.
  - x = 1: β₃ < α₃ forces y > k, and then β₁ = 1 + yθ₁ > α₁.

Both are impossible. ∎

**Corollary 15.7 (PROVED).** If Z[ρ] = O_K, then α ∈ I(K) and t(K) ≥ a.

**CERTIFIED (`r7_cubic.py thomas 3 100 12 40`, data/r7_thomas.jsonl).**
- **Index 1 found for every a.** For each 3 ≤ a ≤ 100, the least b ≥ 2a − 1 with Z[ρ] = O_K (PARI nfinit, index 1) is b = 2a − 1 in 91 cases, b = 2a in 5 cases and b = 2a + 1 in 2 cases.
- **Exact recomputation.** m(α) = a in O_K, with minimiser δ₀ or a·ρ² − a²ρ + 1 (e = 0, w = a = b − a + 1).
- **Box test.** The independent box test (all β with Tr β² < Tr α²) confirms indecomposability for a ≤ 40.
- **Sharpness.** sails gives t(K) = a exactly for a ≤ 12, with ι(K) = a(a − 1) when b = 2a − 1.
- **Consequence.** sup over totally real cubic fields of t(K) ≥ 100. The witness at a = 100 is b = 199, i.e. x³ − 299x² + 19900x − 1, with disc 3881294188177 ≈ 3.9·10¹².

**t₃ = ∞: HEURISTIC.**
- The gap: infinitely many a admitting some b ≥ 2a − 1 with Z[ρ] = O_K.
- disc(a, 2a − 1) = (a² − a − 1)(4a⁴ − 8a³ + 9a² − 5a + 23), and squarefree values of such polynomials are not known unconditionally. Index 1 needs less than a squarefree discriminant, but no unconditional route is known to us.

## 4. Rank r and Kitaoka

**Corollary 15.8 (PROVED).** Suppose a classical rank-r lattice M over K is universal.
- For every δ ∈ O^{∨,+}: #{α ∈ O⁺ : Tr(δα) = 1} ≤ rd. Equality forces δO = 𝔡⁻¹ and M unimodular.
- For K real quadratic, every edge partial quotient satisfies u_E + 1 ≤ 2r, and u_E + 1 ≤ 2r − 1 unless N(μ_E) = −1 and M is unimodular. For ternary forms, u_E ≤ 5.
- Also κ□(K) ≤ s□(M) ≤ 2r·s.

*Proof.* M represents each of the u_E + 1 elements of E as an element, not merely as a class. Apply Theorem 15.1 and Corollary 15.2. ∎

Pairing represented elements against codifferent functionals of trace 1 is the standard way to bound ranks of universal forms from below (Blomer–Kala 1503.05736, Kala–Tinková 2005.12312; IMPORTED as context, not re-read here). The orthonormal-frame form and its equality case above are ours.

**Proposition 15.9 (PROVED; the Blomer–Kala count and CKR IMPORTED).** The indecomposables alone cannot prove Kitaoka's conjecture over real quadratic fields.
- Let n be even with D = n² − 1 squarefree. Then √D = [n − 1; 1, 2n − 2], s = 2 and ι = u₁ = 1.
- So I(K) = {η^k} with η = n + √D, and ⟨1⟩ ⊥ ⟨η⟩ represents every indecomposable.
- Every condition of Corollary 15.8 already holds for r = 2: u_E = 1 on both edges, every supporting functional of value 1 meets at most the 2 points of an edge, and κ□ ≤ 2. A fortiori they hold for r = 3. (For r = 1 the strict edge bound fails, because N(ε) = +1, and indeed no rank-1 lattice represents both 1 and η.)
- Yet by Chan–Kim–Raghavan (1996; IMPORTED, list confirmed by secondary sources) only Q(√2), Q(√3) and Q(√5) carry a classical universal ternary form. D = 15, 35, 143, 195, … carry none.

So a proof of finiteness has to use elements that are not indecomposable (traces ≥ 2 against every δ), or local conditions.

**Cubic data (CERTIFIED, from sails facets).** μ₁(K) := max_δ #{α ∈ O⁺ : Tr(δα) = 1} is at least the largest number of lattice points on a facet of integer distance 1. By Corollary 15.8, μ₁(K) > 9 excludes classical universal ternary forms and μ₁(K) > 6 excludes classical universal binary forms. See §3.3 for the counts.

**Rank 3.**
- N₃(K) ≤ 6s for classical lattices over real quadratic K. The non-classical bound is ½τ₆·s = 36s.
- The exact S(K,3) is OPEN: there is no complete rank-3 enumeration.
