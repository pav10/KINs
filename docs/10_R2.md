## 1. Setting
- D ≡ 3 (mod 4) squarefree, K = Q(√D), O = Z[√D], 𝔭 = (2, 1+√D) with 𝔭² = 2O.
- ε is the fundamental unit; N(ε) = +1 for D ≡ 3 (mod 4).
- E = K(√−2) = Q(√D, √−2), a CM field with complex conjugation ρ (z ↦ z̄).
- **(H)** 2ε ∈ (K^×)². It holds for every prime D ≡ 3 (mod 4): classical, and CERTIFIED for all 1136 such primes D ≤ 20000. It also holds for 579 of the 2919 composite D ≡ 3 (mod 4) with D ≤ 20000.
  Under (H), write μ² = 2ε and θ := (μ/2)√−2. Then θ² = −ε, N_{E/K}(θ) = ε, and E = K(√−ε).
- *Det class 2*: the integral binary lattices M with det M ∈ 2·(K^×)², equivalently K(√−det M) = E.
- For an ideal class c ∈ Cl(O_E) and a squarefree product 𝔫₀ of primes of K that are inert in E, define
  n_{𝔫₀}(c) := #{[γ] ∈ I(K)/(K^×)² : some integral 𝔅 ∈ c has 𝔅𝔅̄ = γ𝔫₀^{-1}O_E}.
  Write n(c) := n_{O}(c).

## 2. Results and proofs

**Lemma 10.1 (PROVED; CERTIFIED for 609 fields).** O_E = O_K ⊕ 𝔭^{-1}√−2, and hence Tr_{E/K}(O_E) = 2O_K. Under (H), O_E = O_K[θ] = O_K[√−ε].
- *Proof.* The standard integral basis of Q(√m, √n) with m ≡ 3, n ≡ 2 (mod 4) is 1, √m, √n, (√n + √(mn))/2. With m = D, n = −2 this gives O_E = Z⟨1, √D, √−2, √−2(1+√D)/2⟩. Since 𝔭^{-1} = O + O(1+√D)/2, this is O_K ⊕ 𝔭^{-1}√−2. Every element X + Y√−2 has X ∈ O_K, so Tr = 2X ∈ 2O_K. Under (H), μ generates 𝔭, so μ/2 generates 𝔭^{-1} and θ = (μ/2)√−2 generates 𝔭^{-1}√−2. ∎
- *Check.* PARI's `nfbasis` agrees for all 609 squarefree D ≡ 3 (mod 4), D ≤ 3000.

**Theorem 10.2 (PROVED).**
- **(a) Ideal lattices are integral.** Let 𝔄 be a fractional O_E-ideal and n ∈ K totally positive with nO_K = N_{E/K}(𝔄). Then M_𝔄 := (𝔄, N_{E/K}/n) is a classically integral, totally positive definite binary O_K-lattice in det class 2.
- **(b) Every det-class-2 lattice embeds in one.** Let M be integral in det class 2 and e ∈ M∖0 with α := Q(e).
  - Put ψ(v) := B(e,v) + √−d·(e∧v), where d = det M in some coordinates and √−d ∈ E.
  - Then M ≅ ψ(M) ⊆ H := (𝔄, N/α) with 𝔄 = ψ(M)O_E, and H is integral.
  - N(𝔄) = α𝔫 for an integral ideal 𝔫 of K, so H = ν·M_𝔄 when 𝔫 = (ν). Every det-2-frame lattice of `data/family_det2.jsonl` has 𝔫 = O.
- **(c) Exact count.** Under (H), γ ∈ Q(M_𝔄) ⇔ some integral 𝔅 ∈ [𝔄]^{-1} has 𝔅𝔅̄ = γO_E. Hence s□(M_𝔄) = n([𝔄]^{-1}).

*Proof of (a).* For CM extensions 𝔄𝔄̄ = N(𝔄)O_E = nO_E. So for z, w ∈ 𝔄 we have z w̄ ∈ nO_E, and B(z,w) = Tr(z w̄)/(2n) ∈ Tr(O_E)/2 = O_K by Lemma 10.1. N(z) ≻ 0 for z ≠ 0, so the form is totally positive. In the coordinates z = X + Y√−2 the Gram matrix is diag(1/n, 2/n), of determinant 2/n². ∎

*Proof of (b).*
1. The bilinear Lagrange identity ψ(v)·ψ(w)̄ = Q(e)·[B(v,w) − √−d·(v∧w)] holds (checked exactly on 5000 random rational instances; it is a polynomial identity). In particular N(ψ(v)) = αQ(v), so ψ is an isometry (M, Q) → (E, N/α).
2. For v, w ∈ M, the element t := √−d·(v∧w) satisfies t² = B(v,w)² − Q(v)Q(w) ∈ O_K. So t ∈ O_E, and therefore ψ(M)ψ(M)̄ ⊆ αO_E.
3. Hence 𝔄𝔄̄ ⊆ αO_E, i.e. N(𝔄) = α𝔫 with 𝔫 integral.
4. Integrality of H follows as in (a): z w̄ ∈ αO_E and Tr(O_E) = 2O_K. ∎

*Proof of (c).*
- (⇒) Take z ∈ 𝔄 with N(z) = γn. Then 𝔅 := z𝔄^{-1} is integral, lies in the class [𝔄]^{-1}, and 𝔅𝔅̄ = N(z)/n · O_E = γO_E.
- (⇐) 𝔄𝔅 = (z) with z ∈ 𝔄, and N(z) = uγn for some u ∈ O_K^×. Since N(z), γ and n are totally positive, u = ε^k. Then z′ = zθ^{-k} ∈ 𝔄 has N(z′) = γn, using N(θ) = ε. ∎

**Corollary 10.3 (PROVED).** Let S_E(K) := max{s□(M) : M integral binary in det class 2}.
- **(i) Upper bound (no hypothesis).** S_E(K) ≤ max_{𝔫₀, c} n_{𝔫₀}(c).
  - *Proof.* By (b), s□(M) ≤ s□(H). Represented γ satisfy γ𝔫^{-1} = N(𝔅) with 𝔅 ∈ [𝔄]^{-1}.
  - Write 𝔫 = 𝔫₀𝔪 with 𝔪 = N(𝔐) a norm of an integral ideal. This is possible because split and ramified primes are norms and inert primes occur to even powers.
  - Then 𝔅𝔐 lies in [𝔄]^{-1}[𝔐] and has norm γ𝔫₀^{-1}.
- **(ii) Lower bounds under (H).** S_E(K) ≥ max_c n(c) ≥ n̄ := Σ_c n(c)/h(E) ≥ N_E(K)/h(E), with N_E(K) := #{[γ] : γO_E is a norm of an integral ideal}. These follow from (c) and pigeonhole.
- **(iii) Equality.** When the groups 𝔫₀ ≠ O are smaller, S_E(K) = max_c n(c). This is CERTIFIED for D = 43, 691, 5419, 12511, 17539, 19231: the other groups reach at most 2, 4, 6, 10, 10, 10, against 8, 20, 28, 36, 36, 34.

## 3. What this explains (the shape of every example with large s□)
- **Even values.** Under (H), multiplication by θ maps M to itself and multiplies Q by ε. Since [γ] ≠ [γε], the represented square classes come in pairs. All 742 det-2 family lattices with (H) have even s□. All 46 odd values occur in fields where (H) fails; in all 727 such lattices, exact ≤ model, as Corollary 10.3(i) predicts.
- **Unit determinant (L2.5, L6.4).** M_𝔄 has det 2/n² ∈ ε·(K^×)², and for unimodular M the Clifford order is O_K[θ], which equals O_E (Lemma 10.1). This is the "det = ε⁺" pattern of the imported extremal lattices.
- **Galois-symmetric frames (L7.6–L7.8)** are the classes fixed by the Galois action; they are often but not always the best class. The det-2 family attains max_c n(c) in 204 of 583 fields.
- **Why E = K(√−2) and not K(i).**
  - For E = K(i), O_E ∋ (1+√−D)/2, which has relative trace 1, so its ideal lattices are only norm-integral.
  - Their hulls give larger upper bounds: 10 at D = 43, 24 at D = 691, 44 at D = 5419, 38 at D = 12511, 56 at D = 17539, 44 at D = 19231.
  - But the classical sublattices are poor: exactly 4 for D = 43, at most 6 for D = 691, at most 10 for D = 5419 (`classical_sublattices`, exact evaluation).
  - For E = K(√−2) the classical condition is free (O_E = Clifford order), and that is where large s□ lives.
  - The D = 43 bound 10 also exceeds the complete R1 value S = 8. For classical lattices, K(i) is a strict upper bound only.

## 5. Records and growth (CERTIFIED records; growth EMPIRICAL)

**Shape (EMPIRICAL).**
- All 146 fields D ≤ 100000 with max_c n(c) ≥ 36 have **D prime** (≡ 3 mod 4). Composite D are only partly covered by (H), and their Cl(E) has larger 2-rank: genus characters spread the indecomposables over more cosets of Cl(E)².
- Every record field has cyclic Cl(E).
- Every certified witness is a free lattice (a binary form) of determinant ε up to squares.

Scope: all 4055 squarefree D ≡ 3 (mod 4) with D ≤ 20000 (1715 satisfy (H)), and all 6040 fields with (H) in 20000 < D ≤ 100000.

Record progression of max_c n(c) (= S_E(K) where Corollary 10.3(iii) is checked):
6 (D = 19), 8 (43), 12 (163), 20 (331), 24 (2731), 28 (5419), 36 (12511), 38 (30871), 40 (44851), 48 (61051), **52 (D = 98179, s = 730)**.

The average n̄ grows slowly, from about 9 near D = 10^4 to about 12 near 10^5, and max_c n(c) runs about 3–4 × n̄.

**Heuristic (HEURISTIC; gap named).**
- n̄ ≈ (#indecomposable square classes that are norms)/h(E), with κ□ ≈ 2ι.
- When h(K) = 1: ι is the sum of the odd-indexed partial quotients and grows like √D·(log D)^{O(1)} on suitable D, while h(E) ≍ h(−8D) ≍ √D·L(1, χ_{−8D}).
- So n̄ can grow like a power of log D along suitable D, and by Corollary 10.3(ii) S(K,2) ≥ n̄ would be unbounded.
- **Gap:** a lower bound for the number of norm-indecomposables against h(−8D) along an explicit infinite family. This is a question about partial quotients versus class numbers of Q(√−2D), now independent of lattices.

**Consequence for the central dichotomy (L5.2).**
- The data no longer support boundedness: records grow 8 → 28 → 36 → 52 as D grows.
- R3(b) is now a concrete analytic question: find K with N_E(K)/h(E) → ∞.
- Proving it would give S(K,2) → ∞ via Corollary 10.3(ii).
